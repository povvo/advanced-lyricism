## 1. Composition role

This layer builds event, viewpoint, image sequence, agency, reveal order, causal movement and lasting consequence.

- It receives concept pressure, voice limits, setting/context and song-form function.
- It produces scene beats, causal transitions, reveal order, omissions and residue for lexical, rhetorical, hook and delivery work.
- The paired template tracks what happens, who can know it, what changes and which image carries the consequence.

## 2. Core craft model

Build event, viewpoint, image sequence, dramatic tension and consequential movement through transition, omission, pressure and consequence.

## Pressure, agency, and opposition
For narrative material define:
- agent/speaker;; visible want or task;
- hidden fear/need when relevant;; concrete place;
- opposing force;; visible/audible action;
- withheld information;; change;
- consequence;; viewpoint.

A character who only receives events may still be a witness, but the section needs a changing relation to those events.

## Cause: trigger vs condition
Do not confuse the last visible trigger with the **underlying cause** or condition.

Ask:
1. What happened immediately before the outcome?
2. What condition made that trigger effective?
3. If the trigger vanished, would the outcome still be likely?
4. Does the attempted fix create another version of the problem?

Useful causal relations between units are equivalent to:
- therefore;; but;
- because;; meanwhile from a viewpoint that changes interpretation.

A run of “and then” can be valid montage, but it does not create causality by itself.

## Sequence is load-bearing
Order matters when it creates a state the next unit depends on.

Test:
- would reversing two units change interpretation?; what becomes irreversible?
- what later reveal reclassifies an earlier detail?; what should the audience know before the speaker?
- which event closes an option?; which consequence has a delay?

### Information timing
Reveal the dangerous fact **early** to create dramatic irony/dread.
Reveal it **late** to create mystery/reclassification.
Repeat it **after cost** to turn information into consequence.

Do not treat reveal timing as decoration; it can change the narrative engine.

## Micro-scening
A short narrative can often use four functions:
1. orientation;
2. action/complication;
3. reversal/climax;
4. aftermath/reflection.

Do not force 4/4/4/4. The transferable rule is **change per unit**. A 2-bar scene can be complete if something enters, acts, and alters the state.

### Optional 16-bar drill scene spine

For a verse that benefits from four linked movements:

1. bars 1–4 establish agent, place, want and immediate pressure;
2. bars 5–8 convert pressure into action, procedure or pursuit;
3. bars 9–12 deliver the decisive choice, reveal, reversal or cost;
4. bars 13–16 leave residue: consequence, changed self-understanding, institutional aftermath or altered hook meaning.

Bar 12 or 16 can carry the strongest reclassification because section boundaries help it land, but never delay the true hinge merely to satisfy a number. A non-linear, fragmentary or scene-cut structure remains valid when the causal thread and viewpoint limits stay legible.

## Lyric camera: image selection, not camera cosplay
Borrow visual scale without writing screenplay directions.

- **wide:** place, crowd, weather, route, architecture, social field;; **medium:** bodies in relation, approach/avoidance, blocking, task;
- **close:** object, hand, face, sound, screen, receipt, stain, gesture, one loaded detail.

Shift distance at meaning turns. “Cinematic” is not a virtue if the image itself is generic.

### One readable event per snapshot
The comics source warns against asking one static panel to perform two sequential actions. Lyric equivalent: when a line contains too many independent actions, the listener cannot form a stable image. Split or choose the decisive moment.

## Externalise the internal
Translate interior pressure into observable leakage:
- fear -> route checking, seating choice, scanning, body tension;; grief -> saved messages, routines, untouched objects;
- guilt -> avoidance, ritual, over-explanation;; anger -> clipped procedure, damaged object, narrowed attention;
- tenderness -> maintenance, memory, precise care.

Naming emotion is allowed. The test is whether the lyric also gives the listener something concrete to perceive.

## Cuts, gaps, and closure
The comic “gutter” contributes a lyric principle: listeners can bridge omitted transitions.

A cut is strong when the adjacency creates:
- cause;; contrast;
- echo;; scale change;
- time jump;; ironic continuity.

Do not explain what the listener can infer. But if the missing step contains the actual causal change, omission becomes confusion.

## Density as sequence pacing
Many quick image-events create montage and pressure.
One sustained image creates decompression and weight.
Silence/rest after a major event can function like an empty or held panel.

Compression and decompression are structural choices, not mere word-count changes.

## Dialogue and subtext
Dialogue earns space when it acts.

Treat dialogue as performable action: a line pressures, conceals, recruits, tests, reassures, deflects, accuses, or changes status between speakers. Read it aloud against the scene action; if the body, image, or situation already communicates the fact, make the words do a different job.

Useful forms:
- non-answer answer;; deflection;
- status move disguised as small talk;; practical sentence carrying grief/threat;
- repeated phrase whose meaning changes;; interruption;
- quoted institutional language against lived reality.

### Redundancy test
If an image/action already proves the emotion, dialogue that simply names it may be redundant. Write the line after mentally staging the action and cut what the performance already communicates.

Avoid:
- maid-and-butler exposition;; therapy-transcript confession with no dramatic pressure;
- every character speaking in the same syntax;; opponents who exist only to recite the song’s thesis.

## Perspective mapping
Possible positions:
- participant;; witness;
- chronicler;; recipient/target;
- outside observer;; retrospective self;
- future/younger self;; institution/collective when justified.

For each position map:
- what can this voice know?; what can it **not** know?
- what does it want us to believe?; what does it misread?
- which detail would only this position notice?; what does another position know that this one cannot?

This preserves the old perspective-mapping emphasis on **blind spots** and perspective-getting rather than generic “switch pronouns.”

A perspective shift must change information, ethics, emotional distance, or causal understanding.

## Consequence continuity
A major event should not be treated as disposable spectacle. Preserve **permanent or lasting consequence** where the story supports it: a major event must leave at least one trace in:
- body;; relationship;
- reputation;; resources;
- routine;; location;
- belief;; language.

This folds “false consequence” and “aesthetic consequence” into lyric craft. Extremity without aftermath becomes decorative escalation.

## Violence and transgression
When part of the brief, ask both:
- **content:** what happens, to whom, by whom?
- **form:** does shock come from explicitness, repetition, blankness, omission, timing, boredom, understatement, or fragmentation?

Form is part of the dramatic and aesthetic decision. Graphic detail earns its place by increasing pressure, consequence or interpretation; quantity alone does not create impact.

## Action as argument
Physical conflict can embody incompatible values, loyalties, fears, or relationships. If an action sequence changes only bodily position and not the underlying pressure, consider what belief is being tested.

Ground large stakes in mundane human stakes when possible. The contrast makes scale legible.

## Impact stack
Build emotional impact in this order:
1. establish the macro arc, thematic pressure and endpoint before polishing isolated images;
2. give the listener an ordinary relationship, routine or desire that can actually be damaged;
3. decide whether information timing creates mystery, dread, irony or retrospective reclassification;
4. make the decisive action test a belief or relationship, not merely raise spectacle;
5. heighten an existing beat by adding the missing setup, choice or aftermath instead of inventing unrelated extremity;
6. shift between intimate detail and large-scale consequence so each makes the other legible;
7. leave residue after the event and let later syntax, behaviour, motif or hook return carry it.

This is the lyric translation of “expand and heighten”: preserve the core emotional destination while repairing dropped threads, weak causality, dated flattening or under-earned consequence. Fidelity is functional, not compulsory chronology.

## Frames as constraints
Letter, testimony, report, diary, interview, trial, voicemail, therapy, instruction manual, and conversation can be useful because each changes:
- who is addressed;; what can be admitted;
- what details count;; what chronology is natural;
- what is withheld.

Use a frame for its information rules, not just its aesthetic label.

## Adaptation / second-draft principle
When converting a memory, prose note, scene, visual reference, or earlier lyric:
1. preserve the core function and pressure;
2. identify dropped thread, weak transition, flat agency, or missing aftermath;
3. heighten the existing emotional beat rather than inventing unrelated spectacle;
4. exploit lyric affordances—sound, compression, repetition, breath, performance;
5. avoid literal transcription.

“Faithful” does not mean chronologically identical. Changing reveal order or compression can improve the engine while preserving the source’s core.

## Anti-patterns
Revise when:
- catastrophic events leave no trace;; the middle repeats activity without changing the plan;
- the speaker only reacts and never chooses/observes differently;; a claimed arc is declared but not demonstrated;
- every line explains motive;; images overload multiple sequential actions;
- all characters share one voice;; “cinematic” wording names shots instead of giving images;
- chronology is preserved even when compression/reordering would increase meaning;
- climax is louder but not more consequential.

### Diagnostic anti-pattern tests

| Failure | Test | Corrective move |
|---|---|---|
| false consequence | remove the crisis: is the later body, relation, routine, resource, belief and language state unchanged? | leave at least one lasting trace even if the broad status quo returns |
| sagging middle | can the middle units swap freely? do setbacks merely add obstacles? | let each setback destroy or revise the current strategy |
| formulaic structure | does the turn happen because the template says so rather than because someone chooses under pressure? | use structure diagnostically and earn turns through agency |
| scope inflation | can a person, subplot, image field or section disappear without changing the spine? | remove or merge non-load-bearing material |
| instrumentalised casualty | does another person's suffering exist only to motivate or decorate the speaker? | give that person agency, specific experience and residue beyond the speaker's reaction |
| declared arc | is change stated but never proven by a costly choice? | stage a choice between the old protection and the newly learned value |
| antagonist as thesis | does opposition only recite the song's argument? | give opposition a coherent want, pressure and method that can genuinely win |
| maid-and-butler exposition | are characters telling each other facts they both know? | make information an instrument of persuasion, concealment, seduction, hurt or status |
| therapy transcript | does the speaker diagnose their own deepest pattern with frictionless accuracy? | externalise it through avoidance, ritual, action, contradiction or a blind spot |
| boundary/reveal failure | is a reveal exposed before the listener reaches the intended section turn? | place setup before the boundary and payoff after it; use silence or return as the lyric page-turn |
| execution before architecture | is surface polish hiding an untested causal spine? | validate signposts, endpoint, reveal order and consequence before micro-polish |
| self-flattering narrator | is the autobiographical speaker exempt from bias, complicity or cost? | grant the narrator a blind spot and a concrete detail that resists their preferred story |
| literal transcription | does chronology preserve facts while losing emotional causality? | compress, combine or reorder while preserving the user's factual boundaries and core pressure |

## Event, state, and transition modelling

Sound-design methodology offers a useful narrative discipline:

**analyse the real event before building the model.**

For a scene:
1. identify observable events;
2. identify state before each event;
3. identify transition rule;
4. identify state after;
5. identify residual trace.

Example abstraction:
`door locked -> key fails -> phone dies -> option closes -> speaker changes plan`

This prevents atmosphere from substituting for causality.

### Envelope thinking
An event often has phases analogous to an envelope:
- attack: entry/trigger;; rise/development;
- sustain: maintained state;; decay/release;
- residue.

Do not force every scene into the same shape. Use the phases to ask where the lyric is spending time.

A common weakness is an overlong attack (setup) and no residue (consequence).

### Procedural rather than encyclopedic detail
Good sound design models the few mechanisms that create the perceptual identity of an event instead of storing every possible recording.

Likewise a lyric scene does not need every fact. Select the **minimum causal/sensory components that recreate the experience in the listener**:
- one location cue;; one action;
- one pressure;; one sensory fact;
- one consequence.

“Store less, process more” becomes: fewer generic details, more interacting details.

### Boundary cues
Scene changes can be signaled by:
- temporal jump;; register shift;
- repeated object returning;; change of direction;
- new agent;; silence;
- syntax reset;; musical boundary.

When several cues align, a short transition can carry large structural weight.

## 4. Decision method

### 4.1. Frame
- Name the section function, hard constraints, and the complete set of craft objectives and questions; record them in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.2. Baseline
- Describe the present behaviour before judging it; Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.3. Hinge
- Identify the full set of words, bars, events, and performance moments involved in the binding issue.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.4. Mechanism
- Explain how the suspected variable creates the observed effect; Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.5. Isolate
- For causal comparison, hold strong dimensions fixed while varying each suspected lever separately; then combine every improvement that survives its isolated test and record the results in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.6. Material and tool support
- Ground the decision in supplied creative material, direct reading or rehearsal, and every relevant representation, detector or generator.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.7. Audition
- Compare baseline and candidate in the medium that matters; Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.8. Collateral
- Check meaning, voice, rhyme, rhythm, performance, sequence, and consequence as relevant.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.9. Commit
- Keep the most effective change set that survives audition without analytical explanation; Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.

### 4.10. Handoff
- Pass observations, locked decisions, open questions, and generated candidates into every connected domain that can use them.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/narrative-scene.md`.
