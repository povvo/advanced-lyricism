# Revision and Evaluation — Deep Reference

> Working template: `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`
> Machine routes: `assets/domain-registry.json` · `assets/tool-catalog.json` · `assets/dependency-manifest.json` · runtime method `references/runtime-orchestration.md`
> This reference contains the domain method; its paired template carries the method through active lyric work.

## 1. Composition role

This layer diagnoses every relevant weakness and opportunity, maps their interactions and priorities, applies a coordinated high-leverage revision set, and routes each finding back to every domain that owns it.

- It receives the current lyric, performance/beat context, locked strengths and all relevant tool observations.
- It produces a defect map, baseline/candidate comparisons, accepted changes and feedback edges into concept, scene, wording, sound, timing, delivery and form.
- The paired template keeps the strongest existing properties protected while upstream repairs propagate through downstream layers.

## 2. Core craft model

Revision maps the whole defect and opportunity field, then coordinates priorities so upstream repairs, local craft and downstream performance reinforce one another.

## Eight passes
1. **Pressure / meaning** — can each line be paraphrased; does it serve the section?
2. **Causality / agency / consequence** — who acts; what changes; what remains afterward?
3. **Lexical system** — concrete words, accurate domain terms, coherent register, motif logic.
4. **Rhyme / phonology** — stressed sound, family quality, internal/end placement, predictability.
5. **Flow / bar line** — entry, accents, density, breath, closure/enjambment, gear changes.
6. **Voice** — viewpoint, syntax, pronunciation, local register, humour, emotional distance.
7. **Freshness / impact** — stock metaphors, obvious rhyme pairs, empty name-drops, uniform image distance.
8. **Performance** — repeated stumbles, emergency breaths, unintended stress, swallowed key words.

Complete every applicable pass. Use the findings to set depth and order: repair upstream causes first, then rerun and deepen every downstream pass affected by those changes.

### UK drill compound pass

When the selected lane is UK drill, inspect the interacting system rather than counting genre tokens:

- does the speaker position and local syntax stay credible without a forced MLE-marker quota?;
- do rhyme pivots vary their tails before suffix repetition becomes predictable?;
- do hard landings and deviations match the beat, breath and narrative function?;
- does each major collision retain a complete surface reading and exact second mechanism?;
- do scene actions produce consequence or residue rather than violence-as-decoration?;
- does the strongest turn land at the best musical/narrative hinge, whether or not that is bar 12 or 16?;
- does the result remain deliverable at the intended BPM with actual consonant and breath load?;
- if feedback history exists, does the candidate respect demonstrated keep/reject patterns without overruling the current brief?

## Out-loud protocol
The original revision system’s strongest invariant survives unchanged in spirit:
1. perform at intended tempo;
2. perform again without looking at the page where possible;
3. mark every repeated stumble;
4. mark involuntary breaths;
5. clap/tap the pulse and note where natural stress fights it;
6. circle words that disappear or blur;
7. revise every repeatable performance failure while retaining one-off observations for comparison;
8. retest.

A one-off stumble may be performance noise. A repeatable stumble identifies a real performance constraint.

## Meaning test
For each clever line:
- what does the surface sentence mean?; what does each added reading mean?; which word supports each reading?; is any layer merely an association or name-drop?
- would the line still work if the listener misses the hidden layer?

If the surface collapses, the wordplay has become annotation-dependent.

## Predictability test
Predictability can enter through:
- repeated orthographic suffix;; same bar ending shape;; same rhyme position;; same sentence length;; same image distance;; same emotional volume;; same reference type.

Do not ban repetition. Ask whether repetition creates pattern, motif, hook identity, or merely exposes the next move.

## Filler test
Delete or rewrite a line if its main function is:
- completing a rhyme family after meaning is exhausted;; restating the previous line without development;
- supplying generic scene language;; maintaining genre costume;; explaining a reference the line failed to make legible.

A connective line can be plain and still necessary if it changes time, place, agency, or breath.

## Anti-pattern library: lyric translations of the visual-narrative failure corpus

### False consequence / aesthetic consequence
Large event, no material/interpersonal/psychological trace. Fix aftermath, not adjective volume.

### Sagging middle
Activity repeats without forcing a new plan, understanding, or cost. Escalate consequence or change strategy.

### Formulaic misapplication
A useful grid, four-part arc, rhyme quota, or “cinematic” rule becomes a template obeyed after its function has disappeared. Restore function; keep only the scaffolding that helps.

### Scope inflation
Too many domains, references, characters, time periods, or ideas compete for one section. Reduce to the pressure-bearing set.

### Reactive speaker
The voice receives plot but reveals no choice, interpretation, or changed stance. Give agency or purposeful witness.

### False arc
The lyric announces growth/damage/loyalty without showing changed behaviour, language, relation, or choice.

### Thesis-only opponent
An “other” exists only to prove the speaker right. Give the opposing position a concrete desire, competence, or cost.

### Expository / on-the-nose speech
Dialogue tells both parties what they already know or labels the emotion instead of acting through it.

### Therapy-transcript drift
Introspection becomes clinically explicit but dramatically inert. Add situation, addressee, resistance, or consequence.

### Voice collapse
Different people, ages, memories, or social contexts speak with identical syntax and reference systems.

### Reveal misplacement
The key information lands before tension exists or after the audience has already inferred it. Move it to change mystery/dread/reclassification.

### Image overload
One line tries to contain several sequential physical actions or incompatible spatial images. Choose the decisive snapshot.

### Shot-list thinking
The lyric says “camera/zoom/scene” instead of supplying perceptible detail. Remove production vocabulary unless it is literally part of the content.

### Grimdark escalation / trauma as spectacle
Extremity keeps increasing because prior extremity stopped working. Restore stakes, human relation, or consequence.

### Execution before architecture
Bars are polished before pressure, viewpoint, or section movement exists. Return to pre-write.

### Writer-proxy immunity
The speaker is always the smartest person in the room and never pays interpretive or material cost. Add blind spot, resistance, or consequence when truthful to the voice.

### Chronological tyranny
Every event is preserved in order even though selection, omission, or reordering would make the actual pressure clearer.

### Rhyme-as-reason
The event exists because the rhyme required it. Replace with a meaningful anchor or change family.

### Technique pile-up
Several advanced devices occupy the same listening moment and compete. Simplify one layer.

### Generic authenticity
Markers declare scene membership without specific syntax, objects, institutions, or experience.

### Reference dependency
The bar is empty without encyclopedic recognition. Restore a surface sentence.

### Tonal reset
A major event occurs, then the next section returns to identical emotional language as if nothing happened.

## Simplification cascade
When multiple symptoms share a root, fix upstream:
- filler lines <- unclear pressure;; forced rhymes <- bad anchor;; voice drift <- wrong viewpoint;; breath failures <- over-dense profile;
- exposition <- weak scene selection;; stale hook return <- no changed context;; repeated extremity <- missing consequence.

One root change can eliminate several patches.

## Candidate selection and expansion
Use a structured revision loop:
1. classify the decision: reversible line tweak or expensive structural change;
2. set a threshold from the brief;
3. widen to 2–5 genuinely different candidates;
4. reality-test them inside the actual bar/section;
5. compare pairwise on meaning, sound, rhythm, voice, freshness, consequence, and performance;
6. note the main downside of the winner;
7. lock the strongest option as the new baseline;
8. continue every implicated craft layer, detector, generator, performance check and downstream comparison, preserving the dimensions that already improved.

Use the threshold as a quality floor while the full revision graph continues through every applicable layer.

## User preference update
Explicit user reactions outrank generic taste. Track what they keep/reject and **why**:
- voice;; content;; rhyme looseness/density;; flow;; emotional distance;; reference density;; image specificity.

Separate stated preference from scene popularity.

## Close-reading revision
Apply anchored close reading at line level:
- identify the exact word/sound/image creating the effect;; distinguish intended effect from inferred intention;
- revise from exact textual features, not genre cliché;; retain ambiguity when multiple readings are supported.

## Mechanical indicators
`scripts/craft_audit.py` measures line/density proxies, repeated endings, pronunciation coverage, and sound repetition. Combine those measurements with close reading, supplied context, beat audition and performance to assess freshness, speaker credibility, causality, microtiming, emotional depth and whether an entendre sustains multiple complete readings.

A metric identifies where to inspect; the integrated craft pass makes the judgement.

## Scorecard
Grade weak / adequate / strong:
- pressure;; specificity;; agency/consequence;; imagery;; rhyme audibility/variety;; rhythmic contour;; voice consistency;; reference precision;
- hook memory;; performance viability.

Keep the dimensions separate rather than averaging them into one percentage. Use the scorecard to set repair order, then address every important weak dimension and the interactions among them.

Record the scorecard, complete defect/opportunity map, interactions, priorities, and coordinated repair set in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

## Executable opportunity pass

After semantic revision but before final polishing, run every tool relevant to the observed risks.

### Bar-line / syntax

## Detect -> audition -> decide

For any flagged opportunity:
1. identify the exact textual, musical or performed feature;
2. state what technique could exploit it;
3. make one local variant;
4. perform/read it;
5. compare meaning and voice;
6. keep or reject.

The detector's purpose is to widen attention, not to prescribe density of technique.

## Rewrite thresholds

### Local polish
Common signals:
- one repeated stumble;; one dead rhyme;; one filler phrase;; one unclear line;; one accidental stress collision.

### Structural rewrite
Common signals:
- the section has no pressure/change;; the speaker's viewpoint drifts;; rhyme dictates meaning throughout;; every line resolves identically;
- no breath or delivery strategy can rescue density;; the hook repeats without changing function;; the scene has no consequence.

A tool should never hide a structural failure by producing more ornament.

## 4. Decision method

### 4.1. Frame
- Name the section function, hard constraints, and the complete set of craft objectives and questions; record them in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.2. Baseline
- Describe the present behaviour before judging it; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.3. Hinge
- Identify the full set of words, bars, events, and performance moments involved in the binding issue; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.4. Mechanism
- Explain how the suspected variable creates the observed effect; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.5. Isolate
- For causal comparison, hold strong dimensions fixed while varying each suspected lever separately; then combine every improvement that survives its isolated test and record the results in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.6. Material and tool support
- Ground the decision in supplied creative material, direct reading or rehearsal, and every relevant representation, detector or generator; record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.7. Audition
- Compare baseline and candidate in the medium that matters; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.8. Collateral
- Check meaning, voice, rhyme, rhythm, performance, sequence, and consequence as relevant; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.9. Commit
- Keep the most effective change set that survives audition without analytical explanation; Record the decision in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

### 4.10. Handoff
- Pass observations, locked decisions, open questions, and revision findings into every connected owning domain; record the handoff in `assets/templates/revision-evaluation.md`.

## 10. Adversarial checks

- What observation would make this diagnosis wrong?; Could the same feature serve a different function here?
- Is a genre prior being treated as a requirement?; Is a grid, dictionary, detector, or score over-trusted?
- Does the change serve the user or merely display technique?; Would the line work if the hidden reference is missed?
- Which strongest existing property must remain untouched?; Can an upstream repair remove several symptoms, and which additional local or cross-layer repairs still compound it?
- Can the result be understood without analytical markup?; If tools disagree, what direct audition or observation can arbitrate?
