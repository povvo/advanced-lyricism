## 1. Composition role

This layer designs recurrence, contrast, listener memory, return variation and section-level information movement.

- It receives the concept invariant, voice, narrative change, lexical/rhyme memory and musical section map.
- It produces hook identity, return rules, section contrast and song-level movement that feed performance and revision.
- The paired template tracks what stays stable, what changes and what each return now means.

## 2. Core craft model

A hook is a stable identity point while verses create change. The skill of song form is managing **return, difference, information, and contrast**.

## Hook function
Stability can come from:
- melody;; rhythm;
- wording;; image;
- harmony;; question/command;
- one rhyme family;; a production gap;
- a repeated social address.

Decide what the listener should recognise before deciding how many words to repeat.

## Archetypes
- **melodic** — pitch/vowel contour carries identity;; **chant** — rhythmic/social participation carries identity;
- **phrase** — one verbal unit is the anchor;; **hybrid** — two or more of the above.

Choose by beat, voice, and dramatic need.

## Genre-conditioned hook modes
These are conditional construction routes, not identity or content requirements.

| Lane | Useful hook prior | What must still be decided from the actual track |
|---|---|---|
| UK drill | compact 2–4-bar loop; chant or phrase identity; low lexical load; gang/response layers and ad-libs can occupy cut-outs; deadpan attack or a small slide can contrast the 808 | whether repetition, stacking, pitch and ad-libs fit the speaker and available space; derive subject matter from the supplied concept and speaker, with violence or gunshot vocabulary available when that lane is actually in the material |
| trap | melodic or hybrid identity; triplet cells; sustained vowels; ad-lib counterlines; vocal/808 contour may mirror or answer | actual key/range, tuning aesthetic, consonant congestion and whether melodic mirroring masks the bass |
| boom bap | rapped, sung, sampled or no conventional hook; strong phrase/call-response identity; contrast may come from sample or density | swing, sample density, rights/availability of any proposed sample, and whether straight bars serve the form better |
| grime | high-energy call/response; crowd-completable phrase; simpler than verse while retaining percussive speed; a restart-worthy first landing | full-time feel, reload/return point, breath, crowd response slot and whether speed reduces recall |

For drill specifically, test one two-bar and one four-bar version. Preserve the shortest version that establishes identity, leaves the 808/drums legible, and survives two returns without needing added filler. Gang vocals and ad-libs are arrangement variables, not proof of genre.

## Compression
Hooks often benefit from:
- fewer simultaneous ideas than verse;; shorter syntax;
- memorable stressed vowels;; one central image/proposition;
- clean participation points;; breath/production space.

Do not write a dense verse and rename it chorus.

## Repetition legitimises—until it stops developing
A repeated phrase can become meaningful through familiarity. But repetition should have a reason:
- identity;; ritual;
- obsession;; proof of a loop;
- participation;; changed context.

If return adds nothing, shorten or alter it.

## Repetition-with-difference
Develop a hook by:
- changing one word while keeping cadence;; keeping words while verse context changes their meaning;
- answering from another viewpoint;; statement <-> question;
- changing pronoun/addressee;; removing or adding a final line;
- changing final stress/landing;; returning the same image after consequence.

Use **sequence + recurrence + variation** as a meaning engine.

## The gap
Negative space can be part of hook identity:
- missing expected word;; production gap;
- response slot;; held vowel;
- stopped phrase;; beat of silence.

The listener should be able to anticipate enough to participate. Strategic silence can be as memorable as another line.

## Drop / return placement
A hook can feel larger because of what happens **before** it:
- density narrows;; phrase length shortens;
- rhyme complexity drops;; one unresolved word is held back;
- drums/bass clear space;; final verse line points toward the hook’s first vowel or rhythm.

The “drop” is relational. If everything is already maximised, there is nowhere to arrive.

## Verse-hook contrast
Contrast can use:
- density;; line length;
- pitch;; entry point;
- rhyme complexity;; specificity;
- pronouns;; register;
- emotional distance;; camera scale;
- amount of silence.

Keep one bridge variable so the song still feels like one system.

## Signposts and endpoint
Know a few **signposts** and a destination even when the route stays flexible.

For a song this may mean:
- opening image;; first irreversible turn;
- hook 1 meaning;; verse 2 complication;
- final hook meaning;; last image.

Do not over-outline every bar.

## Reveal architecture
Information placement can change the form:
- reveal before hook -> hook becomes dread/irony;; reveal after hook -> next return reclassifies it;
- hide one fact until bridge/last verse -> retrospective payoff;; disclose to listener before speaker -> dramatic irony.

A repeated hook is especially powerful when the words stay stable but listener knowledge changes.

## Serial logic for verses
A later verse should not be “more verse 1.” It can:
- reframe;; complicate;
- reverse;; widen/narrow scale;
- reveal cost;; change viewpoint;
- move time;; show a delayed consequence;
- test the same claim in a new domain.

If sections can swap freely, the form may be a playlist of bars rather than a developing song.

## Motif return
Map:
1. first meaning;
2. what happens to the motif;
3. what the listener learns;
4. altered return;
5. final payoff.

Motif can be word, object, number, location, gesture, rhyme family, cadence, or silence.

## Density and decompression
Serial/comics material contributes a transferable pacing idea:
- compressed succession -> speed/pressure;; decompressed single moment -> weight/attention.

Use form to control how long the listener inhabits an image. Do not equate “more words” with “more content.”

## Pattern break
Establish enough repetition that a break is perceptible. Break can occur through:
- rhyme placement;; landing behaviour;
- cadence;; register;
- viewpoint;; harmonic/production space;
- sentence length.

The old fixed “break at bar 4/8/12/16” becomes a function: **pattern first, then meaningful deviation**.

## Long-form frames
Letter, testimony, report, diary, voicemail, conversation, or recurring scene can organise a song if the frame changes information rules. Use form for constraint, not decoration.

Map stable identity, changed context, listener participation, and return variation in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

## Participation, stasis, and nonlinearity
A hook can give the listener a response slot, missing word, held vowel, rhythmic completion, or social address to participate in. Repetition may dramatise stasis, while a changed pronoun, omission, harmony, perspective, or final stress can signal progress.

Song order need not be strictly chronological. Parallel scenes, alternating viewpoints, refrains that interrupt time, and returns whose meaning changes can create multilinear understanding as long as each transition is audible and the listener can reconstruct the governing relation.

## Musical grouping and hook return

Hook craft can borrow one deep musical-structure principle: **identity is perceived through invariance plus change**.

A return can preserve:
- exact words;; rhythm;
- pitch contour;; rhyme family;
- first/last line;; production gap.

And vary:
- pronoun;; final word;
- harmony;; register;
- delivery;; omitted word;
- image referent;; emotional meaning supplied by the verse.

The listener needs enough sameness to recognize the return and enough change for the return to matter.

### Boundary cues
A hook entrance/exit becomes more legible when several cues align:
- production density change;; register/pitch jump;
- drum-pattern change;; repetition restart;
- breath/pause;; syntactic completion;
- new address;; tonal/harmonic change.

`music_structure.py` can locate feature discontinuities from MIDI/audio; treat them as candidate boundaries for audition.

### Continuing thrust
A repeated figure can change character without stopping motion. In lyrics:
- keep cadence, change semantic role;; keep motif, change scale;
- keep last word, change what it refers to;; keep hook rhythm, move the emotional register.

This often produces stronger development than replacing the entire hook.

## 3A. Extended hook/form guidance

### Stable identity versus changed context
- Decide what must remain recognisable across returns.
- Identity may live in wording, cadence, vowel contour, melody, image, gap, or address.
- Change only the dimensions that the new section state has earned.
- A return can be textually identical yet semantically different because the listener now knows more.

### Hook/verse division of labour
- Let the verse create information, pressure, event, or argument; Let the hook compress the central emotional or social identity.
- Do not force the hook to explain every narrative fact; Do not make the verse repeat the hook's job at higher word count.

### Return design
1. Map what the listener knows before each return.
2. Mark the stable hook identity.
3. Choose one changed dimension: pronoun, register, harmony, production, cadence, or one word.
4. Test whether the change is audible without explanation.
5. Preserve enough sameness for recognition.

### Negative space
- Silence can be part of hook identity; A production dropout can function as a recurring phrase boundary.
- A held vowel may carry more memory than an additional internal rhyme; If the hook is dense, simplify the return before adding a new layer.

## 4. Decision method

### 4.1. Frame
- Name the section function, hard constraints, and the complete set of craft objectives and questions; record them in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.2. Baseline
- Describe the present behaviour before judging it; Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.3. Hinge
- Identify the full set of words, bars, events, and performance moments involved in the binding issue.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.4. Mechanism
- Explain how the suspected variable creates the observed effect; Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.5. Isolate
- For causal comparison, hold strong dimensions fixed while varying each suspected lever separately; then combine every improvement that survives its isolated test and record the results in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.6. Material and tool support
- Ground the decision in supplied creative material, direct reading or rehearsal, and every relevant representation, detector or generator.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.7. Audition
- Compare baseline and candidate in the medium that matters; Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.8. Collateral
- Check meaning, voice, rhyme, rhythm, performance, sequence, and consequence as relevant.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.9. Commit
- Keep the most effective change set that survives audition without analytical explanation; Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

### 4.10. Handoff
- Pass observations, locked decisions, open questions, and hook/form outputs into every connected domain that can use them.
- Record the decision in `assets/templates/hook-song-form.md`.

## 9. Failure modes

Repair the named craft variable, audition the result, and recheck every layer it changes.

### Repetition with no changed context

### Verse compressed into hook

### Hook too word-dense for memory

### Every return rewritten beyond recognition

### Central phrase chosen only for rhyme

### No contrast with surrounding section

### Production change ignored

### Melody assumed from text alone

### Hook value judged only by lexical novelty

### Final return adds no perspective
