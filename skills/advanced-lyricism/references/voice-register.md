## 1. Composition role

This layer preserves a stable relation among viewpoint, syntax, pronunciation, social address, emotional distance, humour, local register and what the speaker withholds.

- It receives the speaker brief, supplied lyrics/recordings/references, audience relation, setting and psychological pressure.
- It produces a voice contract, register/code-switch rules, pronunciation choices and distance movement for every writing and performance layer.

## 2. Core craft model



Track:
- viewpoint and knowledge limits;; syntax and clause length;; pronouns/self-reference;; lexical fields;
- pronunciation-dependent rhyme;; humour/irony;; local vocabulary;; social address;
- certainty/hesitation;; taboo threshold;; landing habits;; emotional distance;
- what the speaker refuses to name directly.

Consistency matters more than marker count.

## Preserve supplied voice
When user lyrics exist, extract recurrent properties before rewriting:
- sentence openings;; compression/deletion;; preferred verbs;; local/institutional nouns;
- rhyme looseness;; internal vs ending placement;; cadence entry;; humour style;
- explicit vs implied feeling;; deliberate grammatical irregularity.

Improve the weak mechanism without normalising the writer into generic “good prose.”

## Artist references -> properties
When a user names an artist, translate the reference into observable craft properties:
- vowel/stress relation;; cadence contour;; landing profile;; narrative density;
- hook economy;; image/reference density;; register;; emotional distance;
- delivery.

## Containment register
One practitioner-derived option:
- intense events described factually or procedurally;; low overt editorialising;; understated bravado;; emotion visible through behaviour/detail;
- dark humour through tonal mismatch.

Think: a chronicler can report catastrophe with the cadence of routine information. This position belongs to a speaker and brief that support it.

## Psychological positions
These are lenses, not characters that must appear.

### Chronicler
Controlled observational authority. Selects facts and moves on.

### Witness
Present and specific without necessarily controlling the event. Mundane surrounding detail can sharpen the stakes.

### Confessor
Internal truth anchored in bodily, relational or behavioural detail rather than generic emotion labels.

### Survival / split register
A colder tactical voice coexists with a reflective human one. Useful for compartmentalisation, self-argument, delayed feeling, or code-switching between public/private selves.

## Sublimation as craft
Pressure can be displaced into:
- procedure;; technical mastery;; work;; humour;
- object maintenance;; hyper-specific planning;; ritual;; status language.

This does **not** mean the emotion disappears. It becomes legible through what the speaker does with it. A later break in the procedure can reveal what was being contained.

## Emotional externalisation
When stronger than naming emotion, show its leak:
- fear -> route checking, seating, scanning, body tension;; grief -> saved messages, untouched objects, repeated routines;
- anger -> clipped syntax, narrowed attention, procedural focus;; vulnerability -> exact physical/relational detail;
- shame -> concealment, over-control, displaced joke.

The rule is not “never say I’m sad.” The rule is “do not let the label substitute for concrete embodiment.”

## Integrative complexity / contradiction
Advanced voice can hold two incompatible truths and make both sides concrete:
- pride and regret;; love and control;; loyalty and cost;; ambition and dependence;
- toughness and vigilance.

Do not resolve contradiction merely to make the section tidy.

## Perspective and blind spots
A coherent voice includes what it cannot see.

Ask:
- What does the speaker know first-hand?; What do they infer?; What do they want the listener to believe?; What do they consistently misread?
- What would another person describe differently?; Which detail is invisible from this position?

A blind spot can create dramatic irony, unreliable narration, or later self-correction.

## Truth and specificity
Specificity is stronger than declarations of authenticity. Prefer actual:
- places;; objects;; routines;; institutions;
- routes;; sensory details;; domain procedures;; relationship facts.

## MLE / London registers
Multicultural London English is not a bag of drill slang. Relevant dimensions can include:
- pronouns/self-reference;; topicalisation;; auxiliary/copula patterns;; deletion/compression;
- discourse particles/tags;; intensifiers;; local verbs;; pragmatic function;
- phonology;; rhythm created by syntax.

Think by function:
- address/collective;; action/movement;; territory/location;; institutions;
- money/material status;; evaluation;; discourse rhythm;; euphemism/semantic repurposing.

London youth speech, MLE, grime/drill conventions, and online “roadman” stylisation overlap but are not identical. Use features when supported by the speaker, supplied text, setting, or explicit request. Never enforce a marker quota.

The sociolinguistic research base supports treating MLE as a contact-derived, socially situated variety; research on “roadman” stylisation is an additional warning that online stylisation is not identical to ordinary community speech.

## Code-switching
A shift can mark:
- authority;; intimacy;; public/private self;; family/peer context;
- memory;; humour;; hook/verse boundary;; quotation.

Signal it through more than one feature—syntax, address, lexical field, cadence, or pronunciation—not a single token.

## Euphemistic irony and repurposing
Ordinary/soft vocabulary can frame hard or emotionally charged realities. The effect comes from semantic and tonal mismatch. It only works if the wording is plausible in the speaker’s context.

## Social relation as lyric engine
A line is often an **action toward someone**, not merely a statement.

Ask whether the line:
- challenges;; withholds;; reassures;; performs status;
- seduces;; deflects;; accuses;; recruits;
- distances;; confesses.

Two voices can use identical facts but perform different social acts.

## Anti-patterns
Revise when:
- slang appears at regular intervals like decoration;; every line performs toughness at the same volume;
- vulnerability is generic abstraction;; local language mixes regions without reason;; voice changes only for rhyme;; different speakers collapse into one diction;
- the speaker has no blind spot and never pays interpretive cost;; intentional non-standard language is “corrected” away;
- authenticity is declared rather than demonstrated.

For current scene context, read `context-research.md`.

## Register as social computation

A register is a response to a social situation.

Before selecting local forms, identify:
- addressee;; in-group/out-group relation;; status relation;; institutional setting;
- whether the speaker is performing, confessing, narrating, warning, joking or withholding;; how much context the listener is expected to share.

Then choose syntax and vocabulary that fit that relationship.

### MLE-specific controls
The practitioner lexicon contributes more than words:
- third-person/self-reference with **man**;; topicalisation;; habitual **does**;; auxiliary/copular deletion;; discourse particles/tags;
- compressed shared-context references;; productive polysemy;
- euphemistic understatement;; code-switching between in-group, mixed and institutional registers.

Select these from the actual speaker and context, carrying the syntax, pragmatics, pronunciation and social relation that make them functional.

### Functional MLE lookup
This table maps meanings and ambiguities. Usage varies by place, peer group and time; supplied speaker material takes priority, and current-scene verification belongs in `context-research.md` when freshness matters.

| Function | Candidate forms and working senses |
|---|---|
| self/address | **man** self-reference; **mans** collective/plural; **my guy / my bro / my G / bredrin / breddy** affiliative address; **mandem** peer collective; **yute(s)** younger person; **big man** respect or irony depending on relation |
| movement/action | **pattern** sort/handle; **link** meet; **dip** leave quickly; **move** operate; **step** approach/confront; **hop out** exit a vehicle; **glide / spin** move through an area; **back out** produce/draw; **lack** be caught unprepared; **bun / hold** depend on their object and context |
| territory | **ends / bits** local area; **block** immediate estate/territory; **strip** commercial street context; **yard** home; **bando / T** dealing location; **opp block** rival territory; **gates** a specific local marker |
| roles/relations | **opp(s)** opposition; **paigon / snake** false or disloyal person; **fed(s) / boydem / 12** police labels with different origins and currency; **civilian**, **rider**, **shooter**, **trapper**, **plug**, **fiend** |
| money/material | **P's / bread / paper** money; **bands** thousands; **food / work** drug product in the relevant street or drug-trade context; **brick / bag** amount or product; **drip / ice / Roley / Off-White** status/style; a contrast such as flight/holiday against flat/block/chicken-shop life can carry movement in status without proving it |
| institutions/state | **on road** active outside/in street life; **on tour / bird / can / HMP** prison framings; **touching road** release; **on remand**, **recall**, **probation**, **bail** retain their legal/institutional meanings |
| evaluation | **bare** much/very; **mad** extreme; **peak** adverse; **calm** good/agreed; **cold / sick / lit** impressive; **bait** exposed/obvious; **long** tedious; **dead / wet** require context |
| discourse/rhythm | **still** emphasis/persistence; **innit** tag; **trust / seen / say nuttin / yeah yeah** alignment or acknowledgement; **you know** shared-context bid; **swear down / on my life / wallahi** oath forms whose social/religious weight must not be treated as filler |

Ambiguity is part of the craft. Resolve the local sense before writing:
- **dip** leave / stab;; **bun** smoke / reject / shoot;; **wet** stabbed / unimpressive;
- **ting** person, object, weapon or general placeholder;; **hold** possess / receive;; **bag** money, amount, object or drugs;
- **food/work** ordinary labour/material or drug product;; **dead** lifeless, ended or negatively evaluated.

When the brief explicitly includes violence or weapon-reference language, the lookup also includes:
- action/outcome: **ching, wet, splash, chef, bore, dip, poke, kweng** for stabbing senses; **corn, bun, clap, blast** for shooting senses;
- weapon labels: **ting, wap, mash, skeng, burner, iron, dotty, bells, rambo, zombie, shank, blade, yank, borer**.
- consequence/euphemism: **dropped, sent, gone, X'd, bodied, sleeping**; preserve the distinction among injury, death, removal and mere insult rather than collapsing them.

Select this semantic field when it is present in the supplied material or concept. Verify intended meaning and current/local plausibility, then preserve consequence rather than treating the terms as decorative intensity.

### Register ladder and ad-lib function
Code-switch by relationship and situation:
- in-group speech may carry the fullest shared syntax, compression and local forms;
- mixed register can retain cadence and selected syntax while making referents explicit for a broader listener;
- institutional register may shift toward formal role nouns, legal terms and explicit chronology.

Ad-libs can affirm, imitate sound, address someone, answer a rhetorical prompt, mark an oath, memorialise or release tension. Place them in actual negative space or at a structural turn. Never enforce a fixed “every N bars” rule; density follows beat space, speaker habit and section function.

### Euphemism and tonal mismatch
Soft or ordinary terms can carry hard realities. The craft mechanism is **semantic distance between surface tone and consequence**.

Do not force violent mappings. The transferable method works with:
- bureaucracy vs intimacy;; childish language vs adult responsibility;; domestic routine vs public crisis;; technical procedure vs grief;
- consumer language vs moral cost.

The mismatch is strongest when both readings are exact.

### UK drill delivery positions

These positions can be blended or changed across sections:

- **chronicler** — reports events, logistics and consequences with observational distance;
- **witness** — remains inside the scene but limits judgement and self-explanation;
- **confessor** — admits cost, fear, complicity or contradiction without losing the established syntax;
- **tactical speaker** — communicates control, threat assessment and survival decisions with compressed affect;
- **disaster bulletin** — calm, factual delivery against severe content; the tonal mismatch carries pressure.

The supplied speaker decides whether any position is credible. Deadpan is a performance variable, not a requirement; “UK,” “drill,” or MLE does not by itself determine nationality, emotion, subject matter or slang density.

## Containment and sublimation

Emotional containment is not absence of feeling.

Possible architecture:
1. intense internal state;
2. controlled surface behaviour;
3. detail leaks the state;
4. structure carries what direct declaration does not.

Sublimation turns experience into:
- motif;; scene;; measured syntax;; analogy;
- sequence;; repeated object;; formal constraint.

“The verse is what is built from the feeling” is a useful craft model, but it should not be imposed on a user who wants overt expression.

### Integrative complexity
A mature voice can hold two apparently opposed truths at once.

Techniques:
- claim + resistant detail;; aggressor perspective + consequence;; confidence + observable fear behaviour;; loyalty + cost;
- humour + grief;; distance + bodily detail.

Do not solve the contradiction too quickly. Give each side concrete manifestation.

## Authenticity check

Prefer:
- exact routine;; exact institution;; sensory detail;; temporal detail;
- concrete consequence;; credible ignorance/uncertainty;; local syntax that arises naturally.

Avoid:
- declarations of being “real”;; marker quotas;; generic hardship inventory;; unexplained local vocabulary;
- borrowed biographical facts.

The Hip Hop course's treatment of “realness” and liveness reinforces that authenticity is **situated and assessed in practice**, not reducible to a wordlist.

## 4. Decision method

### 4.1. Frame
- Name the section function, hard constraints, and the complete set of craft objectives and questions; record them in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.2. Baseline
- Describe the present behaviour before judging it; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.3. Hinge
- Identify the full set of words, bars, events, and performance moments involved in the binding issue; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.4. Mechanism
- Explain how the suspected variable creates the observed effect; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.5. Isolate
- For causal comparison, hold strong dimensions fixed while varying each suspected lever separately; then combine every improvement that survives its isolated test and record the results in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.6. Material and tool support
- Ground the decision in supplied creative material, direct reading or rehearsal, and every relevant representation, detector or generator; record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.7. Audition
- Compare baseline and candidate in the medium that matters; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.8. Collateral
- Check meaning, voice, rhyme, rhythm, performance, sequence, and consequence as relevant; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.9. Commit
- Keep the most effective change set that survives audition without analytical explanation; Record the decision in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.

### 4.10. Handoff
- Pass observations, locked decisions, open questions, and voice/register outputs into every connected domain that can use them; record the handoff in `assets/templates/voice-register.md`.
