## Quick operating card

> Use for execution. For rationale, edge cases, cross-domain reasoning and tool interpretation, read the paired deep reference.

Use when the weakness is conceptual, structural, or generative rather than line-level polish.

## Pressure before topic
A topic is a noun; a section needs a force. Reduce the section to one sentence:
- I want X, but Y makes wanting it dangerous.
- X looks like strength until its cost appears.
- Two true descriptions of the same event refuse to reconcile.
- The action that solves the immediate problem creates the next one.
- The same image means one thing before the turn and another afterward.

Everything else is selected against that pressure.

## Technique budget
Craft dimensions are coupled. Keep the coupling; discard fixed quotas.

Typical trade-offs:
- multisyllabic density vs sensory/narrative room;
- internal saturation vs syntactic clarity;
- entendre density vs clean surface meaning;
- rapid cadence vs image complexity;
- explanation vs listener inference;
- reference density vs the space required to establish each reference.

Choose a section bias—**technical**, **content**, or **balanced**—then create contrast. Do not max every axis.

## Semantic fields before rhyme fields
Build 3–5 fields:
1. physical scene/objects;
2. verbs/procedures;
3. emotional pressure or a collision between defensible values;
4. technical/cultural vocabulary native to the subject;
5. counter-field/opposites.

Only then derive sound families. This keeps rhyme from choosing the subject.

## Domain mining
A durable conceit needs operations, not just nouns. Extract:
- objects and roles;
- verbs and procedures;
- rules;
- states/transitions;
- failures;
- measurements;
- jargon;
- visual properties.

Map each item to the subject by a real shared property. If a field yields two strong mappings and six strained ones, use the two; do not force an extended metaphor.

## Collision: two domains -> third meaning
The old collision system becomes a lyric generator:
1. extract exact properties from domain A;
2. choose domain B with structural overlap **and** meaningful contrast;
3. collide objects, verbs, rules, states, or measurements;
4. reject recognition-only name drops;
5. ask what **emergent third property** appears only because the domains met;
6. mine one second-order implication: what else follows if the mapping is true?

Useful contrasts may include mundane/extreme, childhood/adult consequence, luxury/hardship, medicine/emotion, sport/real stakes, bureaucracy/intimacy, geometry/relationship. They are resources, never quotas.

## Inversion: reverse, then interrogate
1. state the obvious assumption;
2. invert it cleanly;
3. resist treating the inversion as automatically clever;
4. ask what constraint or hidden condition would make it true;
5. keep only the consequence/image that survives.

Constraint archaeology belongs inside concept work. Status can signal cost; protection can become confinement; escape can create the trap.

## Contradiction holding
Do not flatten a tension into a slogan. Give both sides concrete support:
- pride / shame;
- tenderness / threat;
- loyalty / cost;
- confidence / vigilance;
- success / dependence;
- survivor logic / moral consequence.

Search for a formulation that explains why both remain true. Contradiction is generative when it changes behaviour, image choice, or section direction.
