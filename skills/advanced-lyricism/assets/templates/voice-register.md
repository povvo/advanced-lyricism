## Quick operating card

> Use for execution. For rationale, edge cases, cross-domain reasoning and tool interpretation, read the paired deep reference.

Voice is not a bag of preferred words. It is a stable relation among viewpoint, syntax, sound, social address, emotional distance, and what the speaker chooses not to say.

## Voice as system
Track:
- viewpoint and knowledge limits;
- syntax and clause length;
- pronouns/self-reference;
- lexical fields;
- pronunciation-dependent rhyme;
- humour/irony;
- local vocabulary;
- social address;
- certainty/hesitation;
- taboo threshold;
- landing habits;
- emotional distance;
- what the speaker refuses to name directly.

Consistency matters more than marker count.

## Preserve supplied voice
When user lyrics exist, extract recurrent properties before rewriting:
- sentence openings;
- compression/deletion;
- preferred verbs;
- local/institutional nouns;
- rhyme looseness;
- internal vs ending placement;
- cadence entry;
- humour style;
- explicit vs implied feeling;
- deliberate grammatical irregularity.

Improve the weak mechanism without normalising the writer into generic “good prose.”

## Artist references -> properties
When a user names an artist, translate the reference into observable craft properties:
- vowel/stress relation;
- cadence contour;
- landing profile;
- narrative density;
- hook economy;
- image/reference density;
- register;
- emotional distance;
- delivery.

Track supplied voice through concrete craft properties: viewpoint, syntax, pronunciation, social address, emotional distance, humour and withheld information.

## Containment register
One available option:
- intense events described factually or procedurally;
- low overt editorialising;
- understated bravado;
- emotion visible through behaviour/detail;
- dark humour through tonal mismatch.

Think: a chronicler can report catastrophe with the cadence of routine information. Use when the speaker/brief supports it; never force it onto every lyric.

## Psychological positions
These are lenses, not characters that must appear.

### Chronicler
Controlled observational authority. Selects facts and moves on.

### Witness
Present and specific without necessarily controlling the event. Mundane surrounding detail can sharpen the stakes.

### Confessor
Internal truth anchored in bodily, relational, or behavioural detail rather than generic emotion labels.

### Survival / split register
A colder tactical voice coexists with a reflective human one. Useful for compartmentalisation, self-argument, delayed feeling, or code-switching between public/private selves.

## Sublimation as craft
Pressure can be displaced into:
- procedure;
- technical mastery;
- work;
- humour;
- object maintenance;
- hyper-specific planning;
- ritual;
- status language.

This does **not** mean the emotion disappears. It becomes legible through what the speaker does with it. A later break in the procedure can reveal what was being contained.

## Emotional externalisation
When stronger than naming emotion, show its leak:
- fear -> route checking, seating, scanning, body tension;
- grief -> saved messages, untouched objects, repeated routines;
- anger -> clipped syntax, narrowed attention, procedural focus;
- vulnerability -> exact physical/relational detail;
- shame -> concealment, over-control, displaced joke.

The rule is not “never say I’m sad.” The rule is “do not let the label substitute for concrete expression.”
